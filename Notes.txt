It works the same way WeaponReach does — via ModifyDefaultComponentsEvent, setting the BLOCKS_ATTACKS data component on all seven vanilla swords (wood through netherite, including copper). That one component gets you right-click-to-block, the block hand animation, durability cost and the axe-disable interaction for free.

So a raised sword halves a frontal hit instead of erasing it, is slower to bring up, and stays knocked aside longer once an axe breaks the guard. The durability formula is the shield's, which bites harder on a sword since swords have far less durability to spend. Damage types in #minecraft:bypasses_shield go through a raised sword too, and it reuses the shield block/break sounds.

Two notes:
- I put the numbers as constants in the class rather than in Config.java, matching WeaponReach. ModifyDefaultComponentsEvent fires during mod loading, so reading config values there isn't reliable. If you want these on the config screen, they'd need a different mechanism (a damage event handler instead of the component) — say the word and I'll do it that way.
- Swords use the generic BLOCK hold pose, not a dedicated blocking model like the old 1.8 sword had. That would need a client-side item model, which I left out.

How it works

Right-click TNT while gliding → it's released as a bomb instead of placed. On the ground, right-click still places TNT exactly as before.

The momentum part is one line:

bomb.setDeltaMovement(player.getDeltaMovement()
        .scale(Config.BOMB_MOMENTUM_TRANSFER.getAsDouble()));

Vanilla's PrimedTnt constructor gives it a small upward hop and a random spin — what a block of TNT does when lit where it stands. That gets overwritten, because a bomb coming off an aircraft wants whatever the aircraft was doing.

The "slows down like real bombs" part needed no code at all. Vanilla already scales a primed TNT's velocity by getAirDrag() = 0.98 every tick and pulls it down at 0.04:

this.setDeltaMovement(this.getDeltaMovement().scale(this.getAirDrag()));

So a bomb released at 1.5 blocks/tick is down to ~1.0 after a second and ~0.67 after two — it carries forward hard at first, then the throw flattens out while gravity takes over. Exactly the arc you described, straight out of vanilla physics.

Hooked into both RightClickItem and RightClickBlock beside the fireball handler, so aiming at a block mid-flight doesn't place it instead.
